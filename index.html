xBloom, plainly

A short guide for a friend with a new machine and no pour-over history. Not affiliated with xBloom.

Start here

What pour-over is. Hot water falls through a bed of ground coffee held in a paper filter. The coffee drips into your cup. The xBloom grinds the beans, heats the water and pours it for you.

Extraction. Water dissolves solids out of the grounds. Take too little and the cup tastes sour and thin. Take too much and it tastes bitter and drying.

The five levers.

  • Grind size. The biggest one. Finer grounds slow the water and extract more. Coarser grounds extract less and drain faster.
  • Temperature. Hotter water dissolves more, faster. Drop it 2-3 °C to soften a harsh cup.
  • Ratio. Coffee weight to water weight. Ratio sets strength. Grind and heat set extraction. Keep them separate in your head.
  • Pours and bloom. Fresh coffee holds gas from roasting. The first small pour wets the bed and lets the gas escape, so the later water soaks in evenly.
  • Agitation. The dock arm vibrates the dripper to mix water through the grounds. It raises extraction. Too much packs fine particles into the filter and stalls the brew.

Fresh beans and a scale. Coffee goes stale within weeks of roasting, and stale coffee does not bloom. Scoops lie, because grind size changes how much fits in one. Weigh everything. The Studio has a scale built in. The Original does not.

Your first brew

xBloom's own published filter baseline, via the Basic Barista Copilot guide.

  1. Weigh 15 g of beans. Use a ratio of 1:16, so 240 ml of water.
  2. Set grind 35-40, grinder 90 RPM, water 90-93 °C, pour pattern spiral.
  3. Pour 1 (the bloom): 45 ml. Rest 30-45 s.
  4. Pour 2: 100 ml.
  5. Pour 3: 95 ml. Drink it, then change one thing next time.

Where to start by roast

Light. Dense beans. Go 2-3 settings finer than the baseline, and keep the water hot.

Medium. Use the baseline as written.

Dark. Gives up its solubles easily. Go coarser to stay clean, and drop the temperature a little.

These are starting points, not recipes. Taste, then move.

Settings explained

In the order the xBloom app asks for them.

Model

Two machines. The Studio grinds on a 1-80 scale, controls grinder speed, has a built-in scale, and holds 946 ml of water. The Original grinds on a 1-30 scale and holds 700 ml.

Tip: pick the right model in the app first. Every other range depends on it.

Dose

How much coffee you grind, in grams. It sets the size of the brew.

Range: 5-18 g on the Studio, matching the Omni Dripper 2 spec. The Original's recipe editor has been reported as locked at 15 g.

Tip: leave it at 15 g until everything else makes sense.

Grind size

The size of the coffee particles. Your main control over taste.

Range: 1-80 on the Studio (1 is fine, 80 is coarse, about 18.75 µm per step). 1-30 on the Original.

Tip: move 3-5 settings at a time. Smaller moves are hard to taste.

Grinder RPM (Studio only)

How fast the burrs turn. It changes the spread of particle sizes.

Range: 60-120 RPM in steps of 10. Low speed gives a wider spread and more body. High speed gives a cleaner cup.

Tip: 90 RPM is a fair middle. Go to 120 if the cup tastes muddy.

Water temperature

How hot the water hits the coffee. Hotter pulls more out.

Range on the Studio: RT (room temperature), then 40-98 °C in 1 °C steps, then BP (boiling point). The Original goes up to 95 °C. You can set a different temperature for each pour.

Tip: 92 °C suits most filter coffee. Drop 2-3 °C if it bites.

Ratio

Coffee to water, written 1:N. 1:16 with a 15 g dose means 240 ml of water.

Range: 1:14 to 1:15 for a stronger cup, 1:16 as standard, 1:17 to 1:18 for a lighter one.

Tip: change the ratio before the grind when the cup tastes flat and weak.

Number of pours

How many times the machine adds water. The first pour is the bloom. There is no separate bloom setting.

Range: up to 9 pours.

Tip: three pours cover almost everything. More pours mostly slow the brew down.

Per-pour volume

How much water each pour adds, in millilitres. The volumes must add up to your total water.

Range: xBloom publishes no limit. Real recipes run 30-120 ml per pour. The dripper holds about 160 ml of liquid, and the Studio's brewer takes about 500 ml per brew from the app.

Tip: bloom with roughly twice the coffee weight, so about 30 ml for a 15 g dose. 45 ml also works.

Flow rate (app only)

How fast the water leaves the nozzle.

Range: 3.0-3.5 ml/s in steps of 0.1. The knobs cannot set it.

Tip: leave it at 3.0 unless the bed keeps flooding.

Pour pattern

The shape the nozzle traces over the bed.

  • Centered. Straight down in one spot.
  • Spiral. Centre outward, then the outer edge back inward.
  • Circular. The largest radius only.

Tip: spiral wets the whole bed. Use it unless you have a reason not to.

Pause after pour

How long the machine waits before the next pour, in seconds.

Range: no published limit. Real recipes show 0 to about 26 s. A bloom rest is normally 30-45 s.

Tip: give the bloom its rest. Later pauses can be short or zero.

Agitation before and after

The dock arm shakes the dripper side to side. Each pour can have a shake before it, after it, both, or neither. The strength of the shake is a machine setting in the app.

Tip: try one shake after the bloom. Add more only if the cup tastes weak.

Cup type

Tells the machine which vessel is on the dock, which sets its overflow threshold.

Set it wrong and the machine stalls and shows WAIT. The Studio wants a carafe over 300 ml and under 100 mm tall. The Omni Dripper 2 page gives 105 mm or less.

Tip: check this first when a brew pauses for no clear reason.

How recipes get onto the machine

  • NFC card. Tap a card on top of the machine. It works with no phone and no internet.
  • Bluetooth. Pair your phone, pick a recipe, tap Start. The app pushes it over.
  • Auto Mode (Studio). Three recipes live on the machine in slots A, B and C, one press each. Replace a slot from the app under Settings, Auto Mode.
  • The knobs. They change grind size and ratio only. Everything else is app-only.

The app can also share a recipe as a link, so you can send one to a friend.

Fix the cup

One change at a time. Move 3-5 grind settings per step, brew again, and taste before you touch anything else.

Channeling

Water carves one fast path through the bed and skips the rest, so the cup tastes sour and bitter at once. Level the bed, switch the pattern from centered to spiral or circular, and cut the agitation.

Stalled drawdown

The bed will not drain. Grind coarser, cut the agitation, and lower the dose so the bed sits shallower.

WAIT, or "Paused, Resume On Device"

This is overflow protection, not a fault. Remove the scale's factory tape, put the cup down before grinding starts, keep it off the machine wall, never lift it mid-brew, and set the correct cup type in the app. Do not use an espresso grind for pour-over.

Grinder overheating

Keep each grind under 30 seconds, and wait 60 seconds between grinds.
